I once came across the idea that once you've grown up enough, you only need two things: health and happiness. It sounds reasonable. Hard to argue with, even.

## The gap between outcome and condition

No matter how much money you make or how many goals you achieve, if your health collapses or joy drains out of your days, none of it feels like much. That's true.

But what bothers me about this framing is that it focuses on the outcome while ignoring what sustains it. Happiness is fragile. Health doesn't maintain itself through willpower alone. Both are shaped by your environment, your relationships, your time, your knowledge — and your financial situation.

Money can't guarantee happiness. That's true. But money can reduce the pressures that erode it. Money can't buy health, but it expands your options for protecting it.

## What actually wears us down

Most of us don't lose our peace because we lack joy. We lose it because we're managing sustained pressure: bills, illness, family obligations, unexpected crises. In many cases, what makes life hard isn't the absence of happiness — it's the absence of resources to absorb difficulty.

Maybe adults don't just need health and happiness. They need the conditions that protect those things against the disruptions life will inevitably bring. Money isn't everything. But it's one of the most important conditions.

## A question worth sitting with

If the financial pressure you're carrying right now disappeared tomorrow — what would change first? That answer usually reveals what you're actually missing.
