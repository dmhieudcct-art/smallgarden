I booked four days in Hội An with a full schedule: lantern-making in the morning, cooking class in the afternoon, pottery village at dusk, boat ride on the Thu Bồn river, walking tour of the ancient town...

Then it rained on the first morning. Not a light drizzle — heavy, sustained rain that soaked through umbrellas and canceled most plans.

I sat in a small café near the Japanese Covered Bridge, watching people pass under umbrellas, and realized that for the first time in a long while, I didn't feel the need to be anywhere else.

## What I actually remember

Not the itinerary items. Instead:

The woman running a pho stall who sat sorting vegetables in the early morning and asked where I was from in a thick Quảng accent. I didn't catch everything she said, but the conversation lasted nearly an hour.

A small alley behind the market that appeared in no travel blog I'd read. Old houses on both sides, cats sleeping on steps, jasmine drifting from somewhere nearby.

An evening sitting along the river watching people release paper lanterns onto the water. I didn't take a photo. I just watched.

## What I learned about travel

Dense itineraries are a way of telling yourself you're "making the most" of your time. But sometimes it's the gaps — an unplanned morning, an afternoon of wandering without direction — where the interesting things actually happen.

Hội An isn't the most beautiful place I've been. But it's where I learned to slow down.

I'll go back. Next time, I'll only book the room.
