I first read *Atomic Habits* in 2022 and found it… fine. Read it again at the start of this year and found myself taking notes constantly.

The change, I think, was in me — not the book.

## What I wrote down

**Identity before outcomes.** Instead of "I want to run every day," say "I'm a runner." Behavior flows from who you believe you are, not from the results you want.

This sounds like a word game, but it genuinely shifts something. When I miss a workout, the question stops being "ugh, I broke my streak" and becomes "what would someone like me do right now?"

**Systems over goals.** Winners and losers often share the same goals. What differs is the system they operate within each day.

I applied this to reading: instead of "read 20 books this year," I built an environment where reading happens naturally — book on the nightstand, no phone in the bedroom, 20 minutes before sleep.

**The two-minute rule.** If a new habit takes less than two minutes, do it now. The goal isn't to complete the task — it's to *start*. Once started, continuing is usually far easier than igniting from zero.

## Where I push back

Clear writes as if every problem can be solved with a good enough system. But sometimes the issue isn't habits — it's circumstances, mental health, or external pressures that no habit stack can fix.

The book is most useful when you're doing okay and want to do better. Less useful when you're genuinely struggling.

## Favorite line

> "You do not rise to the level of your goals. You fall to the level of your systems."

Printed and taped to my monitor.
