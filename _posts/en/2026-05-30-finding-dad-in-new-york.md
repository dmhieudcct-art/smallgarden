*Finding Dad in New York* is a novel by Vietnamese author Nguyen Nhat Anh, telling the story of a boy named Bi — who grows up without a father, and one day decides to find him in the most unimaginable city possible: New York.

## The Story

Bi lives with his mother in a small Vietnamese city. His father left when he was very young, leaving no address, only a photograph taken in front of the Empire State Building. That is the only clue.

When he is older, Bi saves up money and buys a plane ticket to New York — with no concrete plan, not enough English to communicate, and no idea where his father might be in a city of over eight million people. The story follows him through the streets, through chance encounters with strangers, and gradually — not finding his father in the literal sense, but finding something more important.

## What the Book Is Really About

Nguyen Nhat Anh is not writing about a geographical search. He is writing about the process of a child asking himself: *Who am I without a father?* And more broadly: *What can family be if not blood?*

Bi meets people who have nothing to do with him but treat him like family. He learns that the emptiness a father leaves behind doesn't necessarily have to be filled by that same person.

## Writing Style

Nguyen Nhat Anh writes simply, without philosophical ornamentation. That is both his strength and his limitation — his books read quickly, are easy to get into, but rarely leave you with a line you want to underline. With *Finding Dad in New York*, the child's-eye narration suits him better than many of his other books, because it allows raw emotion without the need for explanation.

## Closing

After finishing the book, the question that lingers is: who are you looking for, and are you sure that's who you actually need to find?
