For nearly two years, I kept telling myself I was "about to" automate my workflows. I read articles about Zapier, watched n8n tutorials, bookmarked dozens of Python guides — then closed the tabs and went back to doing everything manually.

The shift happened when I asked a different question: **What's the one task I dread most each week?**

Not "what could I automate" — too broad. But what specific, repetitive task makes me sigh every time I see it on my to-do list.

For me, it was compiling a weekly report from three separate data sources, reformatting it in Google Sheets, then copy-pasting it into an email for the team. About 45 minutes every week. Zero thinking required. Just hands and patience.

## What I did

**Step 1: Write down every manual step.** I opened a text file and logged every action I took — including the obvious ones: "Open Chrome → Go to dashboard X → Click Export → Select this week → Download CSV → Rename file..."

**Step 2: Identify what a computer could do.** Not every step can be automated. But most can — especially anything that repeats exactly the same way each time.

**Step 3: Start with the simplest possible tool.** I didn't start with Python or APIs. I used Google Apps Script — because my data was already in Google Workspace.

After three evenings, the script worked. 45 minutes per week became 0.

## What matters more than the outcome

What I learned wasn't how to write scripts. It was how to *see* my work differently: every repetitive task is a question — "Why am I still doing this by hand?"

I now have a list of eight workflows waiting to be automated. I do one per month, in order. No rush.

*Do you have a task you repeat weekly and dread? That's your starting point.*
