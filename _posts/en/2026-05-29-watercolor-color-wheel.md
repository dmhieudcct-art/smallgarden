Before mixing colors, you need to understand them. And the simplest tool for understanding color is the color wheel — a diagram that organizes the relationships between colors in a circular structure.

What is a color wheel?
The traditional color wheel has 12 colors, starting from 3 primary colors: red, yellow, and blue. Mixing two primaries creates 3 secondary colors: orange, green, and purple. Continuing to mix primaries with secondaries produces 6 tertiary colors.

The color wheel tells you: which colors are opposite (complementary — creating strong contrast), which are neighbors (analogous — creating harmony), and which form a triangle (triadic — balanced and vibrant).

Making your own watercolor color wheel
This is a foundational exercise every watercolor learner should do at least once:
1. Draw 12 evenly spaced circles or squares arranged in a ring on watercolor paper.
2. Fill in the 3 primary colors at the 12 o'clock, 4 o'clock, and 8 o'clock positions (evenly spaced).
3. Mix each pair of primaries to create the secondary colors and fill in the spaces between them.
4. Continue mixing until you have all 12 colors.

The important thing: don't use tube colors for the secondary and tertiary slots — mix them yourself. The mixing process itself is the lesson.
